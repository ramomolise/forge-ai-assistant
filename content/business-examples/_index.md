The useful pattern is simple: customers repeatedly ask the same questions, but
some decisions still need a person. These examples show how to divide the work.

## Motion Foundry Dance Academy

### The problem

Parents and students repeatedly ask Motion Foundry Dance Academy about dance
styles, suitable ages, prices, class times and complimentary trials. Answering
the same questions takes time, while trial approval and uncertain requests must
still go to Ramo.

### The assistant can

- answer from approved class, age, price and timetable information;
- ask only for details missing from the enquiry;
- suggest a suitable class from the reviewed information;
- submit a complimentary trial request; and
- ask Ramo to follow up.

### The assistant cannot

- invent a class, price or time;
- check availability when no availability tool exists;
- confirm a trial booking; or
- say a request or handover succeeded when its tool failed.

### A person takes over when

Ramo takes over when a customer asks for confirmation, the schedule is unclear,
the approved information has no answer or a tool fails. A submitted trial
request is only a request—it is not a confirmed booking.

## A fictional salon

### The problem

Thandi's fictional salon receives repeated messages about services, prices,
opening times and appointment requests. Staff can answer confirmed facts
quickly, but they must still decide whether a requested appointment is
available.

### The assistant can

- explain approved services and prices;
- share confirmed opening times and preparation instructions;
- ask for the service and preferred date;
- submit an appointment request; and
- offer a staff handover.

### The assistant cannot

- promise that a stylist or time is available;
- diagnose a scalp or health condition;
- change a price or apply an unapproved discount; or
- treat an appointment request as a booking.

### A person takes over when

A staff member takes over to confirm appointments, answer health-related
questions, handle complaints and resolve requests not covered by approved salon
information. Test examples use fictional names and placeholder dates; a real
salon must review its own information and rules before use.

## A fictional repair service

### The problem

Kopano Repairs receives messages asking whether an appliance can be repaired,
what a call-out may cost and when a technician can visit. A short first response
helps, but only a technician can assess the fault and confirm the work.

### The assistant can

- list the appliance types the business handles;
- share an approved call-out fee or explain that a quote follows inspection;
- collect the appliance type, suburb and a short fault description; and
- request a technician follow-up.

### The assistant cannot

- diagnose the fault from a message;
- promise a repair price or arrival time; or
- give unsafe electrical instructions.

### A person takes over when

A technician takes over for safety concerns, diagnosis, quotes and visit
confirmation. The assistant should tell the customer to stop using an appliance
when the business's approved safety wording requires it, then pass the enquiry
to a person.
