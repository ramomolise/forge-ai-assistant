Forge AI Assistant was created by **Ramoroa “Ramo” Molise**, a South African
dance educator and founder of Motion Foundry Dance Academy (MFDA).

## Why Forge exists

Forge began as a practical way to improve repetitive customer communication for
a real small business. MFDA is the first case study because it gives the project
a concrete problem to learn from: recurring WhatsApp questions should receive
consistent, reviewed answers, while confirmations and sensitive decisions stay
with a person.

Building Forge is also part of Ramo's practical technology learning journey.
The broader goal is to turn the lessons into understandable, open-source
educational material for South African entrepreneurs—not to present a private
deployment as a product that anyone can copy safely.

## Why the guide starts with understanding

WhatsApp is an important customer channel for many small businesses in South
Africa. Connecting an AI assistant to it also creates responsibilities: customer
messages can contain personal information, automated actions can fail and a
confident answer can still be wrong.

That is why **Build with Hermes ZA**, the learning path inside this project,
explains what each part does before asking a learner to run a command. Security,
restricted tools and human handover are part of the design from the start.

## What is open and what remains private

The Hugo learning site, tutorials, synthetic examples, review checklists and
sanitised beta lessons are open source. The live MFDA configuration, production
prompts, customer conversations, personal information, credentials, private
endpoints, databases and server logs remain private.

Generic reusable components may be published after beta findings have been
sanitised, reviewed and shown to be safe to share. Their eventual publication
will not mean that every business can deploy them unchanged or that a secure
outcome is guaranteed.
